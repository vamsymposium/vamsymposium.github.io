Day 1 · Thursday, February 4

OpenCAL build workshop

Five to ten teams spend the day assembling an OpenCAL volumetric printer from a kit and leave with a working machine. It takes place in the CITRIS Invention Lab in Sutardja Dai Hall and is intended for groups that want to begin volumetric printing in their own laboratory.

Who it is for

Research groups, teaching laboratories, and makerspaces. No prior VAM experience is required; familiarity with optical benches and Python is helpful. Teams of two to four.

What you receive

A calibrated OpenCAL printer, the bill of materials and build documentation, the control and reconstruction software, and a first printed part.

Cost

The organizers are seeking funding to subsidize approximately ten kits at roughly $1,500 each. Final pricing will be announced with the call for applications.

What to bring

One laptop per team with Python installed, closed-toe shoes, and a way to transport a printer roughly the size of a desktop PC. Tools, eye protection, gloves, resin, and vials are provided.

Safety

The workshop uses photopolymer resins, isopropanol, and high-intensity visible-light projectors. Facilitators brief each team before assembly; see the code of conduct for laboratory safety expectations.

After the workshop

Teams join the OpenCAL community channels for support and are invited to report on their first months of printing at the following symposium.

Topic areas

Where the field is moving

Eight topic areas distilled from roughly forty-five papers published between 2024 and 2026. They organize the panels, the poster session, and the reading list below. Posters may be submitted under any topic.

Topic 01

Light engines and holography

Phase-modulated and holographic projection is replacing amplitude-only DMD patterning: higher photon efficiency, diffraction-limited features, and new geometries such as lensless and single-view systems.

Phase light modulatorsHolographic VAMSingle-exposureLensless

Topic 02

Projection computation and inverse design

Pattern generation has become a differentiable inverse problem: physically based ray tracing and wave optics that account for scattering, occlusions, refraction, and chemical blur.

Differentiable renderingWave opticsOverprintingOpen frameworks
7 papers, 4 from 2026 →

Topic 03

Photochemistry and resin design

Oxygen inhibition, exotherm, viscosity, and dose window are the practical limits on fidelity and size. New chemistries widen the window: inhibition control, RAFT, thiol-ene, and self-assembling macromers.

Oxygen inhibitionRAFTLow viscosityDual-color
8 papers, 6 from 2026 →

Topic 04

Process monitoring, metrology, and control

Closing the loop: in-situ imaging to end exposure automatically, map substrates for overprinting, and characterize parts while they print, so results stop depending on the operator.

Auto-exposureIn-situ metrologyClosed loopRepeatability
7 papers, 6 from 2026 →

Topic 05

Scale, throughput, and production

From one part per vial to manufacturing: thermal management for large volumes, droplet-based serial printing, roll-to-roll, hybrid multiscale platforms, and the operations research behind them.

Large volumeSerial productionRoll-to-rollMultiscale
6 papers, 4 from 2026 →

Topic 06

Materials beyond acrylates

Glass, silicones, fluoropolymers, foams, and composites: routes that keep the resin transparent during printing and add function afterward, plus optics-grade surfaces.

8 papers, 4 from 2026 →

Topic 07

Biofabrication

Cell-laden printing at tissue-relevant densities: scattering-aware patterning, refractive-index matching, printing directly inside microfluidic chips, and light-controlled biology after the print.

Volumetric bioprintingXolographyOrgan-on-chipHigh cell density

Open hardware, reproducibility, and education

Making the technology buildable outside the founding labs: open printers and post-processors, open computation frameworks, shared calibration methods, and the community that maintains them.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a virtual option?

Yes, for the Friday symposium. A video link is sent to registered participants who indicate virtual attendance. The Thursday build workshop is in-person only.

Will talks be recorded?

Recording is at each speaker's discretion. Where permission is granted, recordings are posted to the archive after the event.

Can I present a poster?

Yes. The call for posters is open now, and abstracts are due Friday, January 8, 2027.

Do I have to attend both days?

No. The symposium and the build workshop are registered separately. The workshop has limited places allocated by application.

How does the symposium relate to SPIE Photonics West?

The symposium is scheduled adjacent to Photonics West in San Francisco so that attendees can combine both in one trip. See venue and travel.
